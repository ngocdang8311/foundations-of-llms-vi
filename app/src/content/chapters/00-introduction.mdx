This first post is about classical *Natural Language Processing* (NLP), before the LLM era.

***I know what you're thinking***. Studying old-school NLP might feel like a waste of time, as these techniques have largely been superseded by LLMs.

Still, I believe dedicating a few hours to it is worth your time.
First of all, you'll learn important concepts that are still relevant today.
You'll better understand the genealogy of ideas, which is important for developing your *research taste*.
In particular, you'll see more clearly how and why transformers were introduced and rapidly adopted. Finally, this post references a few dozen seminal papers that you must have seen once in your life if you want to be serious about the topic.

In this first chapter, we will follow two important questions that have shaped the development of the field of NLP:

- **How can we build a machine learning model that takes as input a piece of text, given that it has no fixed length?**
- **How can we transfer what a model learned on one task to another, building on the fact that on every NLP task, language is essentially the same?**

To explore these questions, we'll play mostly with two tasks and two datasets:

- the *IMDB movie reviews* for **sentiment classification**
- *WikiText-2* for **language modeling**.

<Note title="Datasets" emoji="📖" variant="info">
- **IMDB** [@maas2011]: a set of 50k movie reviews labeled either positive or negative, 25k for training and 25k for testing. Get it on the Hugging Face Hub at [stanfordnlp/imdb](https://huggingface.co/datasets/stanfordnlp/imdb). Note that it also contains a split made of 50k [unlabeled reviews](https://huggingface.co/datasets/stanfordnlp/imdb/viewer/plain_text/unsupervised).
- **WikiText-2** [@merity2016]: about 2 million training words from Wikipedia articles, with a vocabulary of about 33,000 words. Use the tokenized version (`wikitext-2-v1`): punctuation is already separated by spaces, and rare words are already replaced by an `<unk>` token. Get it on the Hugging Face Hub at [Salesforce/wikitext](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Salesforce/wikitext).

Other useful datasets are:
- **TinyShakespeare**: about 1 million characters of Shakespeare's plays, from [Andrej Karpathy's char-rnn project](https://karpathy.github.io/2015/05/21/rnn-effectiveness/). Used only as a side demo for character-level generation. Get it on the Hugging Face Hub at [jchwenger/tiny_shakespeare](https://huggingface.co/datasets/jchwenger/tiny_shakespeare).
- **text8**: the first 100 MB of a cleaned English Wikipedia dump (originally from [Matt Mahoney](https://mattmahoney.net/dc/textdata.html)), a standard corpus for training word2vec. Get it on the Hugging Face Hub at [afmck/text8](https://huggingface.co/datasets/afmck/text8).
